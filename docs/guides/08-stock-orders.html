Stock, Recipes and Orders

This guide covers keeping track of ingredients: receiving deliveries, recipes, buying, counting, wastage, batches and expiry, reports, and the Orders & Tabs page that lists restaurant orders. It also explains how every sale takes stock off the shelf.

Who uses it
Store keepers, chefs, managers, accountants.
Where to find it
Admin menu → Stock: Stock Overview, Ingredients, Recipes, Receive Stock, Buying, Purchase Orders, Suppliers, Stock Count, Wastage, Batch Tracker, Stock Reports. Admin menu → Restaurant & Bar: Orders & Tabs.
Permission needed
See the table "Who can do what".
Only shown when
The Stock & Inventory module is switched on in Settings → Modules. Recipes and Tables also need the Restaurant Page module.

Column headings and figure cards on the Stock pages have a small ? beside them. Tap it (or click it on a computer) to read what the number counts, where it comes from and which period it covers. On a phone, a What the columns mean list sits above each table.

How a sale takes stock off the shelf

Stock only comes off for items that have a recipe (see recipes). An item with no recipe sells normally and shows Untracked on the till.

  • Food and drink on a tab or an order sent to a station: stock comes off when the Kitchen, Bar or Coffee Bar marks the line Ready, Served, or bumps the ticket. Firing the order does not take stock.
  • Drinks paid for at the till, or on a tab that is settled: the till marks Bar and Coffee Bar drinks as handed over, and stock comes off then.
  • Room service: stock comes off when the charge is posted to the guest's bill, at the moment the order is placed.
  • How much comes off: the recipe quantity for one portion, times the number sold, divided by the yield %. For example 150 g of chicken at 75% yield takes off 200 g.
  • Which batch: the batch that expires first is used first (batches with no expiry date go last, oldest first). The ingredient's total and its average cost update.
  • Sales made before a cost was known: if an ingredient had no cost when it was sold, that sale was recorded at zero cost. When a delivery, a Stock In or a stock count later gives that ingredient a unit cost, the zero-cost sales are costed at that price automatically. If no cost ever arrives, they stay at zero.
  • If there is not enough: the sale is never stopped. The tile on the till stays tappable and shows Low / none on record. The Kitchen, Bar and Coffee Bar can still mark the line Ready or Served. The ingredient's stock goes below zero, and the system records it as an oversell so a manager can reconcile it. The next delivery fills the shortfall first, or fix it at the next stock count.
  • Put back: cancelling before work starts, voiding an order, and 86 of an item that was not started all put the stock back. A refund does not put stock back.
  • Voided or 86'd after cooking started: the ingredients are already gone, so the dish is counted as wastage once. It is not also counted as usage in Stock Reports. Waste is only recorded when stock actually came off the shelf.
  • Other ways stock goes down: wastage, expired or recalled batches, approved stock count shortages, and manual adjustments by a manager.

What the Stock Overview shows

Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Overview. It checks for expired batches each time you open it and removes them from stock.

  • A banner: either "All good! Inventory is healthy — no urgent alerts right now.", or Heads up / Action needed with what needs attention.
  • Cards: Net Sales Today, Open Tabs, Inventory Value, Wastage (30-day total, with today's figure). Net Sales Today is today's paid orders (by the day they were paid) after refunds and voids. When any paid orders were refunded or voided today, the card shows how many and their total. Room service charged to a room is not included. It opens Orders & Tabs filtered to today's settled orders. The other cards open the related page.
  • Needs Attention, in groups you can open: Out of Stock, Expiring Soon (within 3 days), Expired Batches Still Active, Running Low and Pending Reconciliation (room-service charges that were posted without stock being tracked).
  • Recipe Coverage for the Food menu and Drinks menu: how many items have a recipe. More coverage means more accurate stock and cost figures.
  • Top Items Sold Today, Most Wasted Ingredients (30 days), and quick links to Ingredients, Recipes, Batches, Orders, Wastage and Reports.

Out of Stock and Running Low only list ingredients that have had stock before. An ingredient is Low when it is at or below its reorder point (or its minimum quantity if no reorder point is set). Ingredients that never had any stock are not alerts. They appear as one blue line, Opening stock not recorded (N items), with a link record a stock count that opens the Opening stock count. Selling is not affected by that line. The same rule is used on the dashboard, Buying, Ingredients, Stock Reports and the emailed report.

The buttons in Needs Attention (View, Review, Dispose, Process) open the matching page. The Order button on an out-of-stock or low item opens Purchase Orders, where you can create a purchase order to restock. For the full buying steps see Buying.

How to add and change ingredients

Open Admin menu → Stock → Ingredients. Each ingredient keeps a running quantity and an average cost. Stock arrives in batches, each with its own expiry date.

Add an ingredient

  1. Click Add Ingredient.
  2. Fill in Name and Unit (both required). Units are Grams (g), Kilograms (kg), Millilitres (ml), Litres (L), Pieces (pcs), Packets, Boxes, Dozens (12), Slices, Teaspoons (tsp), Tablespoons (tbsp). Optionally set Category.
  3. If the unit is Packets or Boxes (or another container), a box called Pieces in one packet (it reads Pieces in one box for boxes) appears. Type how many single pieces are inside one, for example 6 for 6 sausages in a packet. It is optional. Menu Management then lets staff sell 1 piece and works out the part of the packet. Dozens (12) are always 12, so the box does not show. If you type something that is not a number above 0 you will see "Pieces in one packet must be a number above 0." Menu Management cannot change this number. It only reads it, so set it here. Until it is set, the pieces choice on the menu is greyed out.
  4. Min quantity (low-stock threshold): when stock is at or below this, the ingredient shows Low.
  5. Default yield %: the usable part after trimming or cooking. 100 means no loss. It can be 0.1 to 100.
  6. For buying: Reorder point, Par level (order up to), Preferred supplier and Supplier lead time (days). Notes are optional.
  7. If you buy this item by the crate, case, tray or box, fill in Bought in packs? with the pack name (for example crate) and Units in one pack (this is the pack size, for example 24 for a crate of 24 bottles). Leave both blank if you never buy it in packs. If you type a size but no name, the pack is called pack. A name with no size is ignored. The stock, counts and recipes for the item stay in the Unit you chose above (for example bottles), never in packs.
  8. Click Save. You will see "Ingredient "Chicken breast" added."

Stock quantities are kept to 6 decimal places, so small parts of a packet add up properly: 6 sales of 1 piece from a packet of 6 take exactly 1 packet off. On screen the numbers are shown shorter.

Names are checked as you type. An ingredient name already used by another ingredient is flagged: "A stock item with this name already exists (archived items count too)." If the save is refused, the form stays open with what you typed and the reason shown.

Other actions on each row

  • Edit — change the details. "Ingredient updated."
  • Stock In — see the next section.
  • Batches — the batches for that ingredient. Use Edit Batch to fix an expiry date, alert days or notes ("Batch updated."). Use Discard to write off a batch as expired, recalled or wasted. You are asked "This will subtract the remaining quantity from your total stock and close the batch. Continue?" and then see "Wasted — batch closed and stock adjusted." (or "Expired ...", "Recalled ...").
  • Adjust — a manual correction, for Admin and Manager roles only. Choose Direction (Add stock (+) or Subtract stock (−)), Quantity and a Reason of at least 8 characters, then Save Adjustment. "Manual adjustment recorded." For anything else use a stock count.
  • Archive ("Archive this ingredient?") hides an ingredient: "Ingredient archived." Choose Archived in the status filter to find it, then Restore ("Ingredient restored.") or Delete ("Permanently delete? This cannot be undone."). An ingredient used in any recipe cannot be deleted. An ingredient that still has stock on hand cannot be archived either: "Cannot archive: 5 kg is still in stock. Count it in a stock count or record it as wastage first, so its value is written off properly." Count it or record the wastage first, then archive it.
  • Export to Excel downloads the list.

Use the category and status filters (Low, Critical, Expiring soon, Archived) to narrow the list. The Status column shows OK, Low, Expiring (within 7 days), Expired or Critical (nothing left).

The list shows 10 ingredients per page. The line under the table (for example "Showing 1–10 of 58") and the page numbers count only the ingredients that match your search and filters. Next to the filters the count reads "12 of 58 ingredient(s)" while a filter is on. Search or filter first, then use the page numbers to move through the results.

How to receive stock by hand (Stock In)

  1. On the Ingredients page click Stock In on the row.
  2. Type Quantity received. Fill in Cost per unit (if you leave it blank or 0, the ingredient's current average cost is used), choose the Supplier (or — Other / one-off —), and set Expiry date and Alert me ___ days before expiry if the goods expire.
  3. Click Receive Stock. If the quantity is more than 5 times what you hold, or the expiry date is in the past, the page asks you to confirm.
  4. You will see "Stock received: 5.000 units. New avg cost: MWK 120.00/unit."

A new batch is created with the next batch number, and the ingredient's average cost is recalculated from the old stock and the new delivery. If the ingredient's stock had gone negative (for example it was sold before the delivery was entered), the delivery settles that shortfall first, and the batch holds only what is left over. The batch notes say how much was settled.

A blank or zero cost works only when the ingredient already has an average cost. A brand-new ingredient needs a real cost the first time.

How to receive a delivery with Receive Stock

Open Admin menu → Stock → Receive Stock. It is a normal admin page with three cards: Scanner (scanner on or off, the camera and the barcode box), Delivery details (the supplier and the date received) and Scanned items (what you have scanned, with the Receive into Stock button). It works with a camera or a USB barcode scanner, on a computer, tablet or phone. On a narrow screen the cards stack one under the other.

  1. In the Scanner card, click the Scanner: OFF button so it reads Scanner: ON. The Camera button and the box Type or scan barcode here… appear. The camera needs a secure (https) connection and your permission.
  2. In the Delivery details card, optionally type the supplier in Supplier name (optional) and check Date received (it starts as today).
  3. Scan each product. Each scan adds one pack to the Scanned items card. Scan again to add another pack, or use the plus and minus buttons. Type the Cost per unit on each line (if you leave it blank or 0 the current average cost is used; an ingredient with no average cost yet needs a real cost). If the goods have an expiry date, pick it in Expiry (optional) on that line. The date cannot be before the Date received. Leave it empty if there is no expiry.
  4. If a barcode is not known, Unknown Barcode opens and asks What is this barcode for?:
    • Ingredient — choose the Ingredient, the Pack Size and a Pack Label, and click Save & Add to Batch. If the ingredient already has a pack (for example a crate of 24), the Pack Size and Pack Label fill in by themselves; change them if this barcode is for something different.
    • Item for Sale — type the Item Name, the Sale Price and optionally a Category (blank uses "Retail Items"), and click Register on POS.
  5. Click Receive into Stock (at the bottom of the Scanned items card). You will see a message that the batches were received into stock. A line that could not be saved is listed: "2 received — not saved: (names)".

Each line becomes a new batch, like Stock In by hand. A scanned barcode that is already registered is named on screen, so you can see which item it belongs to, for example "This barcode is already registered to the stock item "…"." Scan a different code or edit that item.

Barcodes on file: view, search, fix or remove a barcode

At the top of Receive Stock, the Barcodes on file card shows how many barcodes are linked. Click it to open the Barcodes on file panel (press Esc or the × to close it). The Ingredients card next to it opens the ingredients list.

  1. In the panel, the Ingredients tab lists each barcode with its ingredient and unit, the pack size and pack label, who added it and when. Type in Search barcode or name… to narrow the list (part of the barcode digits or part of the name). Click Show more to see further results. Click an ingredient name to open the ingredients list.
  2. The POS items with barcodes tab lists the items for sale that have a barcode (item, barcode, category). It is read-only here; change those in Menu Management.
  3. To fix a pack size, click Edit on the row, change Pack size (must be more than zero) or Pack label (up to 50 characters) and click Save.
  4. To remove a wrong link, click Remove, then Remove link to confirm (or Keep it to back out). Only the link between that barcode and the ingredient is deleted. The ingredient, its stock and its history are not touched, and the barcode becomes unknown, so the next scan asks you to link it again.

Only a user with the Admin or Manager role sees the Edit and Remove buttons; everyone else can view and search. Edits and removals are recorded in the activity log, and the next scan uses the new details straight away.

How to build a recipe (link a dish to its ingredients)

A recipe says how much of each ingredient goes into one portion of a menu item. Open Admin menu → Stock → Recipes. Quantities are per portion sold. Stock comes off when the kitchen marks the dish Ready or Served on the station screen (or it is bumped), when drinks are handed over at payment, and immediately when an item is charged to a room.

  1. On the left use All, Food, Drinks or No recipe, or Search menu items..., and click a menu item. Each shows its food cost %.
  2. On the right fill in the ingredient lines: Ingredient, Qty / portion (in the ingredient's own unit, for example 0.05 kg is 50 g) and Yield %. Click Add ingredient line for more lines. If an ingredient is missing click + Add new ingredient (without leaving page) and fill in Name, Category, Unit, Cost / unit.
  3. Watch Total recipe cost, Sale price, Food cost % and Gross margin / portion. Under 30% is healthy, 30 to 40% is acceptable, over 40% means re-price or rework.
  4. Click Save recipe. You will see "Recipe saved with 4 ingredient(s)." Saving with no ingredients removes the recipe: "Recipe removed (no ingredients)."
  5. To remove a recipe click Delete recipe. "Recipe deleted."

You can also set what an item uses from stock on the Menu page, in the item's own form, under What it uses from stock. It is the same recipe as here. There you can enter the amount in the item's own unit (for example 1 each sausage) or in packs when the stock item has a pack size, and you see the stock cost for one sale and what share of the price it is. See the Menu guide.

How to add a supplier

  1. Open Admin menu → Stock → Suppliers and click Add Supplier.
  2. Fill in Supplier name (required, must be unique), Contact person, Phone, Email, Lead time (days), Payment terms, Account ref, Address, Notes, and tick Active.
  3. Click Save Supplier. You will see "Supplier added." or "Supplier updated."

Supplier names must be unique: "A supplier with this name already exists." Each supplier email must be unique too, ignoring capital letters: "Another supplier already uses this email address." Both are checked as you type, and the save is refused again if a duplicate gets through. The form stays open with what you typed and the reason shown.

The list shows Items (ingredients that name this supplier as preferred) and Total purchased. The pencil icon edits a supplier. The power icon deactivates or reactivates it after a confirmation ("Deactivate this supplier? It will be hidden from purchase orders and stock-in pickers. Its history stays." or "Reactivate this supplier so it can be picked again?"), then "Supplier status updated." Deactivating is usually better than deleting: the supplier's history stays.

How to see what to buy and reorder (Buying)

Open Admin menu → Stock → Buying. It lists every ingredient at or below its reorder point (or its minimum quantity if no reorder point is set). Ingredients with neither set are not listed.

  1. Read the summary: Items to reorder, Supplier orders, Est. purchase value.
  2. Each supplier group lists On hand, Reorder pt, Par, On order, Suggested and Est. cost. Suggested is the par level (or twice the reorder point if no par is set) minus what you hold and minus what is already on open purchase orders.
  3. Click Create draft PO to turn the group into a draft purchase order. The button is off for the Unassigned group: give those ingredients a preferred supplier first.

Items bought in packs are suggested in whole packs, rounded up, so the order matches what the supplier delivers. The Suggested column reads Buy 2 crates (48 bottles), and the draft line is ordered per crate at the price of one crate. Buying never suggests part of a pack: if you need 50 bottles and a crate holds 24, it suggests 3 crates.

The top of Buying shows Left to spend this month when a stock budget is set. The amount links to the Budget section.

If nothing is low the page says "Nothing to reorder — every tracked item is above its reorder point."

How to raise, send to a supplier and receive a purchase order

Open Admin menu → Stock → Purchase Orders. Orders move from draft to sent, then partly or fully received, then closed (or cancelled).

  1. Click New PO (or use Create draft PO from Buying). Choose the Supplier, Order date, Expected date and Notes, then Create draft. "Draft purchase order PO-... created."
  2. While it is a draft, use Add line: choose an Ingredient (or — Custom / non-stock —), a Description, Unit, Qty and Unit cost, then Add. "Line added." The bin icon removes a line.
  3. An ingredient that is bought in packs shows two extra boxes. Order in is either the pack (for example crates (24 bottles each)) or the single unit (for example bottles (single)). The Qty box then reads How many crates. Choose the price basis with the second box: per crate or per bottle. Below the boxes you see the total in units, for example 2 crates = 48 bottles · (price) each · line total (amount), so check it before you press Add. The line is then saved in units, so stock, receiving and the budget always count in bottles.
  4. Click Mark as sent ("Mark this PO as sent to the supplier?"). It needs at least one line. "Purchase order marked as sent."
  5. When goods arrive, fill in Receive now, Price on invoice and Expiry on each line you received, then click Receive stock ("Receive the entered quantities into stock?"). "Stock received against purchase order."
    • Price on invoice starts as the price on the order. If the supplier's invoice shows a different price, type the invoice price. The stock is booked at that price. The order itself is not changed.
    • If the order had no price and the item has never had a cost, you must type the price from the invoice, or the line is refused: "(item): enter the price on the supplier's invoice - this item has no price on the order and no average cost yet." Nothing is received until you do.
    • For a line ordered in packs (for example crates), the box next to Receive now says crates or bottles: choose what you are typing. The box next to the price says per crate or per bottle. When you change a choice, the number already typed is converted for you, so 2 crates becomes 48 bottles. Stock always goes in as bottles.
  6. If more arrived than was ordered, you can still type the larger number, because the stock is really there. Fill in the box Over-delivery note (needed if above outstanding) under that line, at least 5 characters, saying why. The line is then flagged over-delivered with your note.
  7. The order becomes partly received until every line is complete, then received. It only says Received when every line is in: one line over-delivered does not hide a short line.
  8. Click Close when finished (outstanding lines will not be received) or Cancel for a draft or sent order. Print prints the order. You will see "Purchase order cancelled." or "Purchase order closed."

If an order would take its budget over, the order page warns you before it is sent. See How to set and watch the stock budget.

If the order has already received stock, Cancel is refused: "A purchase order that has received stock cannot be cancelled. Close it instead." Only a sent or partly received order can be closed: "Only a sent or partly received purchase order can be closed." The system checks that the change really happened, so you never see "cancelled" or "closed" when nothing changed.

A draft order has not gone to the supplier yet, so it cannot be received and it does not count as On order in Buying. Only Sent and Partially received orders count as on order and accept stock. Receiving creates stock batches linked to the supplier and the order. Lines for custom or non-stock items are recorded but add no stock. Once an order is received, closed or cancelled its lines cannot be changed.

How to set and watch the stock budget (Budget)

Open Admin menu → Stock → Budget. To look at it you need Stock Budget (view). To set budgets, and to approve purchase orders that go over a budget that needs approval, you need Stock Budget (set & approve). Managers have both.

The budget is the most you plan to spend on stock in a month. It is counted in what the stock costs you, not in selling prices.

The card for the month has four figures:

  • Budget: the amount set for the month. It shows Not set until you set one. A % of sales line with no sales to measure yet shows a dash and No sales basis yet.
  • Received: the stock booked in this month, at the cost it was received for. This includes lines with no sales basis yet.
  • Already ordered: what is still to arrive on purchase orders you have sent. This includes lines with no sales basis yet.
  • Left to spend: the budget minus Received minus Already ordered. Orders you have sent are counted straight away, so two people cannot both spend the same last amount. If some lines have no sales basis yet, the card says Budget minus received and ordered on lines with a budget: those lines are left out of Left to spend, but their spending still shows under Received and Already ordered.

Under the figures a bar shows Share of the month gone (how much of the budget is used) next to how far through the month we are. Month-end forecast is what you will spend by the end of the month at this pace. It starts once 7 days of the month have gone. Before that the page says "Too early to tell" and does not judge the pace. Typical messages:

  • "Over budget by ..." when the money is used up.
  • "... left to spend - N% of the budget is used." when 80% or more is used.
  • "Ahead of pace - at this rate you'll spend ... by month end." when the forecast is above the budget.
  • "On track." when none of the above applies.
  • "Too early in the month to tell how spending is going." or the label "Too early to tell", in the first 7 days of the month.
  • "No sales basis yet - a % of sales budget needs some food and drink sales to measure against, so it is not checked until there are." on a % of sales line with no sales to measure yet.

Fixed amount or % of expected sales. A line can be a fixed amount, or a percentage of the food and drink sales we expect for the month, before VAT. The till prices include VAT, so the page takes the VAT out before it works out the budget. Expected sales are this month's sales so far spread over the whole month, once 7 days of the month have gone. In the first 7 days of a month, or before any sale that month, last month's full sales are used. A past month uses what was actually sold. If there is no sales figure to measure against yet, the line shows No sales basis yet. It is not counted as over, and it never holds up a purchase order. Its spending still shows under Received and Already ordered.

Stock costs are used exactly as you enter them on receiving and on purchase orders. If you enter costs with VAT, budget with VAT.

What the budget does not show yet. Returns to a supplier are not recorded yet, so spending on the budget only goes up. If the supplier's price is different from the price on the purchase order, the budget shows the real amount only when the goods are received, not when the order is sent. Long lists in the card's detail panel show the latest entries and say "Showing the latest N of M". The total above the list still counts all of them.

Each line also says what happens when an order would go over it:

  • Warn if over: the order can still be sent, but the person sending it must tick a box first.
  • Needs approval: only someone with Stock Budget (set & approve) can send it, and must give a reason of at least 5 characters.
  1. Click Add a line, choose Whole stock (all categories) or a category, then pick Fixed amount or the percentage option and type the amount.
  2. Choose Warn if over or Needs approval.
  3. Click Save budgets. You will see "Budgets saved." Nothing is saved until you press it. If nothing changed you see "No changes to save."

To save time use Copy last month (fills the form with last month's lines) or Fill from last 3 months (the average monthly receipts of the last 3 months). The form fills in and you check it: "Filled from what was received in the last 3 months (average per month). Change anything you like, then press Save budgets." If there is nothing to copy you see "Last month has no budget to copy", or "No stock received in the last 3 months". Past months are closed and cannot be changed.

Where else you see it. The Stock Overview shows a Stock budget tile with its bar. When 80% is used it shows "Most of this month's stock budget is used", and when it is over it shows "Over budget this month". The Buying page shows Left to spend this month and warns if the order you are building would go over it.

When a purchase order would go over budget

The budget is checked when you press Mark as sent on a purchase order, and again if you make a sent order bigger. Nothing is sent until the check is passed.

  • Warn line: the order page shows the box "I know this order goes over budget and I want to send it anyway". If you do not tick it you see "This order takes the budget over for [category]. Tick the box to send it anyway."
  • Needs approval line, without the approve permission: the button is locked and the page says "This order takes the budget over and needs approval. A budget manager has to send it." If you try anyway you see "This order takes the budget over for [category]. A budget manager has to approve it before it can be sent."
  • Needs approval line, with the approve permission: a box asks "Why is it going over budget?". Type a reason of at least 5 characters. Without one you see "This order takes the budget over for [category]. Give a reason (at least 5 characters) to approve it."
  • When the order is sent over budget you see "Purchase order marked as sent. It is over budget and that has been recorded." The order then shows Went over budget - approved by your name and the reason, and every change is in the budget's history.

Stock already received is never blocked by the budget. The budget only stops new orders from being sent.

If the budget page says "You do not have permission to set stock budgets.", you have Stock Budget (view) only. Ask a manager to set the budget or to approve the order.

How to do a stock count

Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Count. Every shortage must be explained, and a big shortage (and every opening count) must be approved by someone other than the person who counted, unless an administrator has switched the second-person setting off (see the note after the steps). Service can carry on while you count: sales, deliveries and wastage recorded during the count are taken into account.

  1. Under Start a count choose What to count: Everything, One category (then pick a Category) or Only the items I choose (hold Ctrl or Cmd to pick more than one). Optionally add a Label (for example Morning or Closing) and Notes.
  2. Click Start count. The system saves the quantity it expects for each ingredient at that moment. "Count ... started. Enter your physical readings."
  3. Every Counted box starts blank and the line is Not counted. Enter what you physically counted for every line. 0 is a valid count when the shelf really is empty, but a blank box is never read as 0. The count is blind for the person who started it, and for anyone who is not a manager: the page says Blind count. and hides the In stock at start, Should be there, Difference and Value columns until the count is submitted (or approved, for non-managers). A manager who did not start the count sees the Should be there column, which is what the system expects you to have at the moment you counted that line (the starting figure plus anything sold, delivered or wasted since). Difference and Value are measured against it.
  4. For every shortage choose a Reason: Spillage, Expired, Staff meal, Sampling, Prep waste, Theft suspected, Correction or Other, and add Notes if useful. The filter Not counted only finds lines you have missed.
  5. Click Save readings ("Count readings saved.").
  6. Click Submit for approval ("Submit count for approval? Once submitted you cannot edit it. Every line must have a reading."). It is refused while any line is not counted. "Count submitted for approval. Shortage cost: MWK ..." Leave Email the approvers ticked to email everyone who can approve. The count then shows Waiting for approval and who can approve it; Remind approvers sends the email again (once every 10 minutes). If your button reads Submit & correct stock instead, the setting is Off: see the note below.
  7. When the setting is On (the default), someone with Approve stock counts (admins and managers have it) opens the count and clicks Approve & correct stock ("Approve this count? Stock will be corrected to match what was counted. This cannot be undone.") or Reject count (type a reason of at least 8 characters). A different person must approve an opening count or a big shortage, unless an administrator has switched that rule off (see the Stock Loss Prevention guide).
  8. On approval the differences are applied to stock, with the approver's name and time kept. "Count ... approved. Variances applied to stock." A rejected count shows the reason. Click Count again to enter the readings again, or Delete count to remove it (see the rules below).

When the setting is Off. The second-person setting is Stock count: a second person must approve in Hotel Settings (Settings → Booking Settings → Hotel details & policies). Only an administrator can change it. When it is Off, the count button reads Submit & correct stock ("Submit this count and correct stock now? Stock will be changed to match what was counted. This cannot be undone. Every line must have a reading."). One press submits and approves the count, so stock is corrected at once. There is no Email the approvers box and nobody else has to press anything. ("Count ... submitted and approved. Variances applied to stock.")

If one of the approval checks fails, the count stays Waiting for approval and the page shows the reason. Fix what it names, then press Approve & correct stock. If the count has gone back to being counted with a yellow Sent back box, add the reasons and press Submit & correct stock again. Nothing is changed until the count is approved.

Rules for counts

  • A different approver for big shortages (when the setting is On): a shortage that is over both the cost limit and the percentage limit (set in the stock variance settings) cannot be approved by the person who counted. The page shows "This count has shortages over the approval threshold, so it must be approved by a different manager or admin than the person who counted it."
  • Only the counter or a manager can save readings, submit a count or count it again.
  • A count cannot be approved if another count was approved after its lines were counted: if another count that covers some of the same items was approved after you counted them, approving this one too would add the same stock twice. The page refuses: "This count is out of date: another count was approved after these items were counted - ... Approving this one too would add the same stock twice. Use "Reject count", then "Count again" to take a fresh stock snapshot and count again." Click Reject count (type a reason of at least 8 characters), then Count again, and count the lines again. Nothing is posted to stock until a count is approved.
  • Big counts save normally: a count with hundreds of lines now saves in one go. The old 406 Not Acceptable error on Save readings no longer appears. If you still see it, reload the page and save again, then tell your administrator.
  • Counting again starts over: Count again takes a fresh snapshot of the stock and every line goes back to Not counted, so enter every reading again.
  • Deleting a count: a count can be deleted only while it is still being counted, or after it was rejected. Use Delete this count (a count being counted) or Delete count (a rejected count), or Delete on its row in the Counts list. It never changed stock, so nothing is put back, and its lines go with it. A count waiting for approval or approved can never be deleted.
  • Who can delete a count: a count still being counted can be deleted by the person who started it, an administrator, or someone with Approve stock counts. A rejected count can be deleted by an administrator, or by someone with Approve stock counts who did not count it. Every delete is kept in the activity log. The page then says "Unfinished count ... deleted. Stock was not changed." or "Rejected count ... deleted. Stock was not changed."
  • A shortage that appears at approval: if sales or waste recorded while you counted turn a line into a shortage with no reason, nothing is posted. The count goes back to draft with a yellow Sent back box naming the lines, and your readings are kept. Add the reasons and submit again.
  • Write-offs are capped: if a shortage is bigger than the stock still on the shelf, only what is there is written off, and the note on the stock record says how much was uncovered.

The page also shows Missing stock (last 30 days), a list of the ingredients losing stock to shortages or manual adjustments that no sale or wastage explains, then Stock added by hand (last 30 days) (stock added by hand, which can hide an earlier loss, with who added it, when and why), and Counts, the list of every count with its status (Being counted, Waiting for approval, Approved or Rejected). Where a figure is zero the page shows None. Surpluses need no reason.

  • Shading: on an open count, a shortage worth at least the minimum value is shaded orange when it reaches the alert at variance percentage of the expected stock, and red when it reaches the needs approval above percentage. A yellow box above the lines says how many are affected, for example "2 lines over the alert level." The percentages are set in Hotel Settings.
  • Count reminder: when no count has been approved for the number of days set in Hotel Settings, the top of the page and of Stock Overview shows "No stock count approved for 9 days — start one." Click start one to go to Start a Count.

See also Stopping stock going missing (anti-theft): every control that guards your stock, a weekly routine for managers, and what to do when stock is short.

How to do the Opening stock count (first-time set-up)

Do this once, when you start using stock, so every ingredient has a starting quantity and cost. Until then, ingredients that never had stock show as Opening stock not recorded (N items). You need Stock Count & Variance, and a different manager or admin must approve it (unless an administrator has switched the second-person rule off in Hotel Settings). It sets stock quantities and costs only. No supplier receipts are made. You can count everything at once, or in parts: an opening count can cover Everything, One category or Only the items I choose, so you can count the items that already have a cost first and the rest later. Items that are not in a count keep the stock they have now.

  1. Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Count. (Or click record a stock count in the blue line on Stock Overview.)
  2. Under Start a count, tick the box Opening stock count (first set-up). Click the ? next to it for a short reminder. Then choose What to count: Everything, One category (then pick a Category) or Only the items I choose.
  3. Click Start count. You will see "Opening stock count ... started for everything (N items). Enter the quantity AND the unit cost for every item you have; someone other than you must approve it. Items that are not in this count are not changed." The words after "started for" name what you chose: everything, the category, or the chosen items.
  4. For each ingredient, type what you physically have in Counted. Type 0 for an empty shelf.
  5. For every ingredient you have in stock, type the price you paid per unit in Unit cost. Without a cost, stock value and food cost would start at zero.
  6. Click Save readings.
  7. Click Submit for approval (or Submit & correct stock if the second-person setting is Off, which approves the count at once). The count is refused until every counted line has a unit cost above zero.
  8. When the setting is On, ask a different manager or admin (not the person who counted) to open the count and click Approve & correct stock. If costs are missing, they click Reject count with a reason, and the counter adds the costs and submits again. When the setting is Off, a missing cost leaves the count Waiting for approval with the reason. Add the cost, then press Approve & correct stock.
  9. After approval the quantities and costs are in stock, and the Opening stock not recorded line goes away for those items.

Counting the rest later. You can run more than one opening count. An item can be in only one open opening count (one still being counted, or waiting for approval). If you start a count that includes an item already in another open opening count, the start is refused and the page names the items and the other count, for example "Not started: 2 of these items are already in another opening stock count that is still open (...)." Either finish or delete that other count first, or choose Only the items I choose and leave those items out. To remove an opening count you started by mistake, see Delete this count in the rules above.

Opening batches with expiry dates. If the opening stock came from a stock sheet that lists expiry dates, the count can carry those batches too. They are not shown on the count page while you count. They become real stock batches only when a different manager or admin approves the count. The counted quantity is then split across them, earliest expiry first, so the expiry dates and the alerts are right from the start. Any quantity left over goes into one plain batch with no expiry date.

Quantities and costs on the count are always in the ingredient's stock unit, even when the item is bought in packs. Type the unit cost per unit (for example per bottle), not per crate.

How to record wastage (spoiled or thrown-away food)

  1. Open Admin menu → Stock → Wastage.
  2. Under Record wastage check the Date (today or earlier; see the note below if a stock count is open). On each row choose the ingredient (Select ingredient...), type the Quantity, and choose a reason: Spoiled, Breakage, Over-prepared, Customer return, Trim/peel waste, Burnt, Spilled or Other. Click Add row for more.
  3. Click Save wastage. You will see "3 wastage entry(ies) recorded."

If you record wastage with a past date while a stock count is open for that ingredient, the entry keeps its date for reports but is booked today in the stock records, so the count does not take it off twice. You see "Some entries were dated for reports but booked now in the stock ledger because a stock count is open for those ingredients."

Not all wastage is typed in by hand. These also appear in the log and the Wastage report:

  • Expired batches, with the reason "Expired batch ...", at the batch's own cost.
  • Recalled batches, with a reason starting "Recall:".
  • Dishes voided or 86'd after the kitchen started them, counted once as wastage and not also as usage.
  • Mark Wasted and Discard on a batch, at the batch's cost.

The stock comes off at once at the real cost of the batches used. Recent entries lists date, ingredient, quantity, cost, reason and who recorded it. The cards at the top (This month wastage, Last month, Days since last entry) open a detail window. A banner reminds you if no wastage has been logged for some days.

How to deal with expiring and recalled batches (see which stock expires soon)

Open Admin menu → Stock → Batch Tracker. Each time the page opens, active batches past their expiry date are removed from stock automatically: "3 batch(es) automatically marked as expired."

  1. Use the cards Expiring ≤ 3 days (with Value at risk), Expiring 4–7 days, Expiring 8–30 days and Total active batches, or the filters (ingredient, status Active, Depleted, Expired, Wasted, Recalled, All, and expiry range).
  2. For spoiled or damaged stock click Mark Wasted, give a Reason, and click Confirm Wastage. "Batch marked as wasted." It is logged as wastage at the batch's own cost.
  3. For a supplier problem click Recall, give the Recall reason, and click Confirm Recall. "Batch recalled and removed from stock."
  4. Receive Stock at the top takes you to the delivery page.

Discarding a batch from the Ingredients page (Batches → Discard) is for the Admin and Manager roles only and needs a reason of at least 8 characters. Batches that expire and are removed automatically also appear in Wastage.

How to read Stock Reports

Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Reports. Tabs: Inventory, Stock-In, Usage, Wastage, Yield, Adjustments, Expiry.

  • Inventory and Expiry are a snapshot of now. The other tabs cover a date range (the last 30 days to start with): set From and To and click Apply.
  • Usage and Yield follow the same rule as the rest of the system: a dish voided or 86'd after cooking started counts as wastage, not usage. The Wastage tab includes expired batches and recalls.
  • Every tab has a CSV download and a "how to read this report" hint with an example.
  • Columns: Inventory (Ingredient, Category, Current qty, Cost / unit, Stock value, Active batches); Stock-In (Date, Ingredient, Batch, Qty, Unit cost, Total, Supplier, By); Usage (Ingredient, Quantity used, Cost); Wastage (Date, Ingredient, Qty, Cost, Reason, By); Yield (Ingredient, Used, Wasted, Expired, Yield %, Loss cost); Adjustments (When, Ingredient, Source, change in quantity, Reason, By); Expiry (Batch, Ingredient, Expiry date, Days left, Remaining, Status).

How to use Orders & Tabs

Open Admin menu → Restaurant & Bar → Orders & Tabs (page title Restaurant Orders). Orders are taken at the POS Till, not on this page. This page is the office view of all orders.

  • Top cards: Orders Today, Revenue Today, Cash, Mobile Money, Card, Voids and Needs Review. Click a card to filter the list. End-of-Shift has a Run Count link to Stock Count.
  • Operational Insights: revenue and orders against yesterday, average ticket, live open orders (walk-in and room service), cash, mobile and card collected, voids today, peak hour and cashier totals. Click a card for detail.
  • Recent Orders table: Reference, Type, Customer, Table, Table Status, Room / Folio, Items, Total, Payment, Status, Health, Cashier, Created, Actions. Filter chips show what is applied, with Clear filters.

Buttons on each row: two or three buttons are shown, and Cancel, Void and Delete are in the More menu at the end of the row.

  • Timeline (the order lifecycle) — the order's full history from placing to paying, with the kitchen steps, payments and stock movements, so you can trace one order. Items — the lines. Receipt — for paid, voided, cancelled and room-service orders. Review — for orders whose items, payments and room charges do not balance.
  • Open and Settle — on an open order. Settle takes payment exactly as the till does (same deals, discounts, split bills and ledger records): it shows the order total, any deal that applies right now, your discount, the split and To collect now. Choose Cash (with the amount tendered and the change), Mobile Money (provider and reference) or Card (manual / slip) (last 4 digits and auth code). Optionally add a Tip, a Discount amount with a reason (needs POS Discounts) and Split bill 2–10 ways; for a split, each person pays in turn and the window stays open for the next payment. If some kitchen items were never bumped, people with POS Force-Serve Tab can tick the box to mark them served and settle. A tab already part-paid continues with its next payment. Settling needs POS Take Payment & Settle and POS Till (Touch); it is not offered for room-service orders (they go to the guest's bill).
  • Cancel — an open, unpaid order that nobody has started. Asks to confirm, then "Order ORD-... cancelled and stock restored."
  • Void — Admin and Manager roles only, for open or paid orders (including an open order the kitchen has started). A reason of at least 8 characters is required. "Order ORD-... voided. Stock restored. Reason logged."
  • Delete (in More) — needs the Delete Orders permission. It removes any order for good, at any time. Type a reason of at least 8 characters. Payments are removed, room-service charges come off the guest bill (with a credit note if already paid), stock is put back only for items the kitchen had not started, prepared food is recorded as wastage, and the cash drawer is not changed. It is refused if a credit note is linked to the order: void or refund instead. Full steps: How to delete an order.
  • Reconcile (in the review window) — Admin and Manager roles only. "Order reconciled: ..."

All of these happen in place: the message appears at the top and the cards and the list refresh without the page reloading.

Act on many orders at once

Tick the box on each order, or the box in the header for every order on the page. A bar appears with how many are selected: Cancel (open, unpaid, not started), Void (Admin and Manager; one reason for all), Delete (needs Delete Orders; one reason for all), Reconcile (Admin and Manager; orders that need review) and Export (the selected rows as a CSV file). Each order goes through the same checks as its own button; the result says how many were done and why any were skipped.

The Receipt page can be printed, saved as PDF or emailed to the guest. Manual consolidation on it lets a manager add a discount (with a reason of at least 4 characters) or a service charge before payment. Once paid, totals are final.

Rules the system enforces

  • Selling is never blocked by stock. Sales and station actions go ahead at zero stock, and the stock goes below zero until the next delivery or count. Wastage and manual subtractions are still refused if they are more than the stock on record.
  • Out of Stock and Running Low alerts only appear for ingredients that have had stock. Never-stocked ingredients show as Opening stock not recorded (N items).
  • An opening stock count needs a unit cost on every counted line and, unless an administrator has switched the second-person rule off, a different approver than the counter.
  • An item can be in only one open opening count at a time. An open count is one still being counted or waiting for approval.
  • Receiving more than a purchase order line expects needs a written over-delivery note.
  • Items with no cost cannot be received by barcode, and a count that adds stock for an ingredient with no cost cannot be approved. Set the cost first.
  • Manual adjustments are for Admin and Manager roles only and need a reason of at least 8 characters.
  • Every stock count shortage needs a reason and a manager's approval. The approved count cannot be undone.
  • An ingredient used in a recipe cannot be deleted; archive it instead.
  • Expired batches are removed from stock automatically when the Stock Overview or Batch Tracker opens.
  • A purchase order's lines can only change while it is a draft. Receiving is only for sent or partly received orders. You cannot receive more than is outstanding.
  • Supplier names must be different from each other.
  • Wastage cannot be dated in the future.
  • A stock count cannot be submitted or approved until every line has a reading. 0 counts as a reading, blank does not.
  • A big shortage on a count needs an approver other than the person who counted.
  • A zero or blank delivery cost uses the average cost, and is refused when there is none.
  • A delivery fills any negative stock first.
  • Draft purchase orders cannot be received and are not counted as on order.
  • Discarding a batch needs the Admin or Manager role and a reason of at least 8 characters.
  • A paid order's totals cannot be changed. Use refund or void.
