Stopping stock going missing (anti-theft)

This guide shows how the system helps you spot and stop stock theft, shrinkage and other losses: missing stock, stock loss, shortages and pilferage. It explains each protection, a weekly routine for managers, and what to do when the shelf does not match the books. It does not name a thief. It shows you where the stock went and who touched it.

Who uses it
Owners, managers and administrators. Store keepers and chefs only need the parts about counting and wastage.
Where to find it
Admin menu → Stock: Stock Count (with Theft Radar), Stock Reports, Wastage, Batch Tracker, Ingredients, Purchase Orders, Stock Overview. Also Admin menu → Restaurant & Bar → Orders & Tabs, and Admin menu → Money → POS Accounting.
Permission needed
See the table "Who can do what" below. Approving, rejecting and manual changes are for the Admin and Manager roles only.
Only shown when
The Stock & Inventory module is switched on in Settings → Modules.

Managers have all of the stock permissions by default. Restaurant staff, chefs and bar staff can take orders and bump tickets, but they cannot count, adjust, write off or approve anything.

How the system protects your stock

Each protection below works on its own. Together they mean that stock cannot leave the shelf in the books without a sale, a wastage entry, an expiry, or a count that a manager has approved.

Every sale takes stock off by itself

For any menu item that has a recipe, the system takes the ingredients off stock when the food or drink is made or handed over, and when a room-service charge is posted. Nobody types it in, so a sale cannot "forget" to use stock. If an order is cancelled or voided before work starts, the stock goes back. Who: automatic. Where: Recipes (build them) and Stock Overview → Recipe Coverage (see how many items are covered). An item with no recipe shows Untracked on the till and takes no stock, so give your selling items recipes.

Food thrown away after cooking started is wastage

If a dish is voided or 86'd after the kitchen or bar has started it, the ingredients are gone. The system does not put them back. It records the dish as wastage once, with its cost, so the loss is visible and not hidden. Who: automatic. Where: Stock Reports → Wastage.

Wastage must be written down, with a reason and a name

Anything thrown away is recorded on Wastage with the ingredient, quantity and a reason (Spoiled, Breakage, Over-prepared, Customer return, Trim/peel waste, Burnt, Spilled or Other). The stock comes off at once at the real cost, and the list shows who recorded it. You cannot record more than the stock on record, and you cannot date it in the future. Who: anyone with Wastage Log. Where: Stock → Wastage, and the Wastage tab in Stock Reports.

Stock is used oldest-expiry first, and expired stock is written off

Each delivery is a batch with its own cost and expiry date. A sale uses the batch that expires first. When the Stock Overview or Batch Tracker opens, any batch past its expiry date is removed from stock and recorded as wastage, so old stock cannot quietly vanish. Who: automatic. Where: Batch Tracker.

Writing off a batch or adjusting stock by hand is for managers only

The Adjust button on an ingredient, and Discard, Mark Wasted and Recall on a batch, are limited to the Admin and Manager roles. Each needs a written reason of at least 8 characters. A subtraction cannot be bigger than the stock on record. Every change is saved with the person's name. Anyone else sees Manager only. Where: Ingredients and Batch Tracker. See the changes in Stock Reports → Adjustments.

Stock counts compare the shelf with the books

A count records what is really on the shelf and measures it against what the system expects. You can count everything, one category, or a few items (a "spot check"). A spot check or a single category is a quick way to count by surprise. Where: Stock → Stock Count.

The count is blind for the person counting

While a count is open, the person who started it does not see the expected quantity, the variance or the cost of any shortage. They must write down what they really see. Staff who are not managers cannot see these figures until the count is approved. The page says Blind count. A manager who did not start the count sees the expected figures, so can check readings as they come in.

Expected stock is worked out at the moment of counting

The column Expected at count time is the stock at the start of the count plus anything sold, delivered or wasted since. A sale in the middle of a count therefore cannot hide a loss, and cannot be counted twice. The final change posted to stock is the difference from this figure.

Every shortage needs a reason

For each line that is short you must pick a Reason: Spillage, Expired, Staff meal, Sampling, Prep waste, Theft suspected, Correction or Other. You cannot submit the count without them. If sales during the count turn a line into a shortage that has no reason, nothing is posted and the count goes back to the counter. A blank line is never read as zero: a count cannot be submitted until every line has a reading.

The counter cannot approve a big shortage

A shortage is "big" when it is worth at least the cost limit and is at least the percentage limit of the expected stock (set in Hotel Settings, see Setting it up). A big shortage cannot be approved by the person who counted. A different manager or admin must do it. The opening stock count always needs a different approver.

Lines are shaded for the approver by one simple rule. A shortage worth at least the minimum value (see Setting it up) is shaded orange when it is the alert at variance share of the expected stock or more, and red when it is the needs approval above share or more. Red needs a second approver. When any line is orange or red, a yellow box above the lines says, for example, "2 lines over the alert level." ("1 line over the alert level." for one). Surpluses and small shortages are never shaded. The shading and the box do not show on a blind count, only to people who may see the figures.

Approval is final, and a rejected count starts again from scratch

When a manager clicks Approve & post variances, the differences are applied to stock and the approver's name and time are kept. It cannot be undone. Reject & send back needs a reason of at least 8 characters. After a rejection, Reopen for re-count takes a fresh snapshot and puts every line back to Not counted, so old readings cannot be tweaked to fit.

Theft Radar points to the ingredients to watch

Theft Radar (last 30 days) sits on the Stock Count page. It is a list of up to 20 ingredients that lost stock in the last 30 days through count shortages or manual subtractions, which no sale or wastage entry explains. Each row shows the ingredient, its Shortage and Manual out amounts in small print, and the cost lost on the right, biggest cost first. If nothing is found you see "No unaccounted shortages detected. All stock movement traces to sales or recorded events." Anyone who can open Stock Count can see it.

Manual additions are listed too, because an addition can hide a loss

Under Theft Radar, the card Manual additions (last 30 days) lists stock that someone added by hand, not received from a supplier and not found in a count. A manual addition can cover up an earlier loss, so compare this list with Theft Radar. Each row shows the ingredient, how much was added, who did it and when, the reason they gave, and the cost on the right. It shows the biggest 20 by cost. If there are none you see "No manual additions in the last 30 days."

The system reminds you to count

When no stock count has been approved for a while, an amber notice appears at the top of the Stock Overview and the Stock Count page, for example "No stock count approved for 9 days — start one." Click start one to go to the count form. If no count has ever been approved it says "No stock count has been approved yet". You choose the number of days in Hotel Settings (see Setting it up). It is only a notice on the screen: no email is sent.

The Stock Overview lists what needs a manager's eyes

Managers and administrators see a Needs review list on the Stock Overview (a red number shows how many). It gathers, from the last 7 days: counts that were approved with a big shortage (Big shortage), stock changed by hand (Manual adjustment), and batches that were written off, recalled or expired. Each row says what happened, who did it and when. Click Look to open the count or the Adjustments report. Click Dismiss to hide one row, or Dismiss all to hide the lot. Dismissing only hides it for you. Other managers still see it, and nothing is deleted from the trail. The list shows the newest 20. Other staff do not see this list.

Deliveries are checked against the order

Receive against a purchase order, not from memory. You cannot receive more than is outstanding unless you type an Over-delivery note of at least 5 characters, and the line is flagged over-delivered with your note. Items with no cost cannot be received. On Stock In by hand, a quantity more than 5 times what you hold, or an expiry date in the past, makes you confirm. Each received batch shows who received it. Where: Purchase Orders and Receive Stock; see the Stock-In tab in Stock Reports.

Buying is held to a budget

A monthly stock budget can warn about, or block, a purchase order that goes over. A line set to Needs approval can only be sent by someone with Stock Budget (set & approve), who must give a reason. The reason and the person are saved on the order. Comparing what was received with what was sold is how you spot buying that does not match usage. Where: Stock → Budget.

Till and cash controls protect stock and money

  • Void: a paid order can only be voided by a manager or admin, with a reason of at least 8 characters. It is saved with their name.
  • Refund: needs POS Refunds and a reason of at least 5 characters. A refund does not put stock back, so a manager must check it.
  • 86 an item: needs POS Quick-86 Items.
  • Discounts: need POS Discounts. A reason can be chosen, and one discount per tab.
  • Delete an order: needs Delete Orders, a reason, and leaves a permanent deletion record.
  • Shift close: the cashier counts the drawer without seeing the expected amounts. A shift that does not balance cannot be closed without a supervisor who has POS Shift Override and gives a reason.
  • History: on Orders & Tabs, Timeline shows each order's full history. The cards Voids and Needs Review filter the list.

The accounts show the cost of losses

On the Accounting Dashboard, the margin panel (named F&B margin, or POS margin if there is no restaurant) shows Stock losses (shrinkage) and a fold-out Loss & folio detail that splits it into wastage, count variance, expiry and recall. Net F&B contribution is the margin after those losses. Where: Admin menu → Money → Accounting.

Alerts on the Stock Overview

The Stock Overview shows Heads up or Action needed, and the groups Out of Stock, Running Low, Expired Batches Still Active and Pending Reconciliation. It also shows Most Wasted Ingredients (30 days). A sale at zero stock is never blocked, but it pushes stock below zero and appears in Pending Reconciliation so that someone looks at it.

Everything leaves a trail

Every stock movement is saved with the ingredient, the amount, the cost, the reason, the source and the person. Order events (placed, paid, voided and so on) are saved with who did them. You can read the stock trail in Stock Reports → Adjustments. The Source column shows where each change came from: for example pos_order or room_service (a sale), void_restore (stock put back), wastage, expiry, recall, variance (an approved count) and manual (an Adjust).

Weekly routine for managers

Set aside about 30 minutes, at the same time each week.

  1. Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Overview and look at Needs review. Click Look on each row, then Dismiss it when you have dealt with it.
  2. Open Admin menu → Stock → Stock Count. Read Theft Radar (last 30 days) and Manual additions (last 30 days). Write down the top ingredients and their costs.
  3. Start a count without warning. Under Start a Count choose By category or Spot check (specific items), pick the ingredients that Theft Radar and your high-cost items point to (spirits, meat, oil, cleaning supplies), and give it a Shift label such as "Surprise". Ask a storekeeper or a different manager to do the counting. Click Start count.
  4. When the count is submitted, open it and review the shortages. Look at the Reason given on each line. Be wary of Other, Correction and Staff meal on high-cost items. Approve it, or use Reject & send back with a reason if the figures do not make sense.
  5. Open Stock Reports → Wastage. Set From and To to the last 7 days and click Apply. Look for entries that are large, repeated, all by one person, or of an item that is not normally wasted.
  6. Open Stock Reports → Adjustments for the same dates. Look at every manual entry. Ask the person named in By if you do not know why it was made.
  7. Open Admin menu → Money → POS Accounting, pick each day and open the Z-report (shift close report). Check Voids, Refund / Void Payouts and any shift marked SHORTFALL or Override applied. On Orders & Tabs click the Voids card and open Timeline on anything unusual.
  8. Open Stock Overview. Clear Pending Reconciliation, deal with Expired Batches Still Active, and check Recipe Coverage.
  9. Open Stock → Budget. Compare Received for the month with your sales. If you are buying much more than you are selling, find out why.
  10. Check the Accounting Dashboard for Stock losses (shrinkage). If it is rising, look at the Loss & folio detail to see whether it is wastage, count variance or expiry.

When stock is short

Use this when a count, Theft Radar or a chef tells you an ingredient is missing. Work down the list and stop when you find the cause.

  1. Name the ingredient and the dates. Open Stock Reports → Adjustments, set From and To, click Apply, and read every entry for that ingredient. Use the CSV button if you want to filter it in a spreadsheet.
  2. Check the sales side. The Usage tab shows what your sales should have used, worked out from the recipes. If the shelf has fallen by clearly more than this plus the recorded wastage, the extra left without a sale.
  3. Check the recipes. If a recipe is too small, the system will take off too little per portion, and a "shortage" will build up on every count. Open Recipes, check the quantity per portion and the Yield %. Also check Recipe Coverage: items sold with no recipe use stock that the system does not see.
  4. Check the Wastage and Yield tabs for the same dates. Compare Wasted and Expired with what the kitchen reports.
  5. Check deliveries on the Stock-In tab and on the purchase order. Was the quantity received the same as the delivery note? Look for lines flagged over-delivered and for deliveries booked with no purchase order.
  6. Check manual changes. In the Adjustments tab, a manual entry that adds stock can cover up an earlier loss, and one that subtracts stock moves it out without a sale. Read the reason and the name.
  7. Check voids and refunds. On Orders & Tabs look at Voids, then Timeline. A paid order that was voided, or a refund (which does not put stock back), can leave the shelf short. Check the Z-report for the cashier and shift.
  8. Check for unrecorded breaks and spoilage. Ask the store and kitchen if anything was thrown away, and record it on Wastage so the books tell the truth.
  9. Run a fresh spot count of just that ingredient, using a different person. Compare it with the earlier figure.
  10. If you still cannot explain it, approve the count with the right reason (use Theft suspected if that is your honest view). The loss is then written off at the batch's cost, appears in Theft Radar and in the accounts as count variance, and the approver's name is kept. Then act on it using your normal staff procedure: the system only provides the evidence.

Setting it up

Variance limits

  1. Open Admin menu → Settings → Hotel Settings → Hotel details & policies and find the group Restaurant & stock. You need the Hotel Settings permission.
  2. Stock count: alert at variance (%): default 2. A counted shortage this size or bigger is shaded orange and counted in the box "N lines over the alert level".
  3. Stock count: needs approval above (%): default 10. A shortage at or above this share of the expected stock is shaded red and needs a second approver.
  4. Stock count: ignore variances worth less than: default 5000 (MWK). A shortage worth less than this is never shaded and never needs a second approver.
  5. Remind to count stock after (days, 0 = off): default 7. When no count has been approved for this many days, a notice appears on the Stock Overview and Stock Count pages. Type 0 to switch the reminder off.
  6. A different approver is needed only when both the approval limit and the minimum value are passed. To make the rule apply to smaller shortages, lower both numbers.
  7. Click Save Hotel Details at the bottom. Nothing is saved until you do.

Permissions, so the counter is not the approver

  • Give Stock Count & Variance to the storekeeper or chef who counts. Do not give them the Manager role. They can then count and submit, but they cannot approve or reject.
  • Keep at least two people with the Admin or Manager role, so a big shortage found by one manager can be approved by the other.
  • Give Wastage Log to the people who really throw stock away, and Stock Reports only to managers and the accountant.
  • Keep Ingredients & Recipes (deliveries, purchase orders) with the person who buys, and have someone else do the counts. Whoever receives stock should not also be the only person who counts it.

Who should have manual adjustments

The Adjust button and batch write-offs are tied to the Admin and Manager roles, not to a ticked permission. Do not make someone a Manager only to give them this. Keep the number of managers small, and look at the Adjustments tab every week.

Staff permissions to keep tight

  • POS Void Tabs, POS Refunds, POS Discounts, POS Shift Override and Delete Orders only for managers.
  • Tills that should not handle money: give POS Till (Touch) without POS Take Payment & Settle.

The steps for giving a person permissions are in Staff & Access.

What the system cannot do for you

  • It does not make anyone count on a schedule. It shows a reminder on the screen when no count has been approved for a while, but you have to start the counts. It sends no emails.
  • It cannot tell theft from a mistake. Theft suspected is a label that a person chooses.
  • It cannot see stock that leaves with no record at all, until a count finds it.
  • It does not track sold items that have no recipe.
  • It cannot stop a manager from approving a count they should not. This is why you keep at least two approvers and read the Adjustments tab.
